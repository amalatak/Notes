## Mass Production

Big business men remained concerned of innovation and efficiency. In the mid-19th century, mass production of cheap materials began under national advertising campaigns. Interchangeable parts began in the 1890s for the bicycle. Henry Ford created the moving assembly line for his cars based on meatpackers disassembly line. 

The first industrial revolution had been in textiles, iron and steel, railroads, and oil. The second (or elaboration) was in consumer goods, which worked by manufacturing similar parts and assembling to a finished product; early on they didn't have precision to make them identical so there was a bottleneck in the "fitters", bringing the parts together. Singer sewing machines had travelling salesman, national advertising, and they began the "pay by installments". Pope's bicycle furthered with absolute interchangeability in bike parts due to improvements in alloys and machine tooling. He also pioneered the stratified market (products for wealthy and poor). In the bike craze of the 1890s, roads were poor. Wright brothers (first flight) were bike repairmen. Ford capitalized on the bikes, constantly modernizing and focusing on interchangeability (capitalism about turning luxuries into affordability). Cars created jobs everywhere for all the stuff that went into it. Soon everything moved to mass production. 

Meanwhile, efficiency experts began scientific management to increase productivity through wellbeing at work. Work was getting really boring and monotonous so to deal with the attrition and turnover, Ford paid his workers way better (2.5x better), maintaining stable workforces and stimulating demand of his cars (empowers workers to be consumers). Scientific management concerned itself with making jobs better, a guy timed operations in a factory and made workers more productive. Hawthorne experiment studied psychological aspects of factory work to see how it affected productivity. 

Advertising developed along side mass manufacturing. Convince a customer that they're impoverished or that they have a problem (create the need), and then offer a solution. Use celebrity endorsements, sex appeal. Sometimes it took up almost the whole budget. Buying on credit became a thing.  

## World War I

Europe went to war in 1914 and Americans remained aloof. The allies were Britain, France, Russia, and Italy fighting against Germany, Austro-Hungary, and Turkey. Germany was facing a two-front war. It was characterized by trenches and defense war, offense hadn't caught up yet. Wilson administration wanted to remain neutral (to preside over an enlightened peace treaty) but gradually became sympathetic of Britain and France and American banks, farmers, and trade profited greatly off the war. 

Initially Americans were isolationist and some pacifists. For most of the war, Britain and France dominated the Atlantic, and the British actually confiscated American shipping to Germany, forcing the sale of the goods (why they put up with it). Submarines were terrifying. Early on they would warn ships and then fire, but then Britain armed merchant ships and German ships no longer surfaced to warn the ships, seen as a horrible violation of war. The sinking of the Lusitania swung popular support greatly towards the Alliance and Wilson sent an extremely angry letter to Germany; and though Wilson was elected on a slogan of "he kept us out of the war", the German decision to declare unrestricted submarine warfare led Wilson to declare War. The small American army deployed and trained rapidly.

After 1898 America began taking a more active role globally, taking over the Philippines from Spain where a guerrilla war took place on both sides. Roosevelt aided the Panamanian revolution (see above), greatly helping the Navy. Americans intervened in the Mexican revolution, seizing Vera Cruz at one point, they crossed the border to get poncho villa. Many Americans thought the US should have a colonial empire. Still they remained isolationist. 

War profiteering off of the allies gave sympathies to England. The American west was particularly against the war, but the Zimmerman letter (offering to help Mexico take back much of the West), consolidated support across the country. The home reception was diverse; the country didn't accept conscientious objectors and there was much propaganda. Wilson preached this as a war to end all wars and save democracy. Congress passed laws to restrict anti-war speech; the only real opposition was socialists believing it was a capitalist war (this is what Eugene Debs went to prison for), marking this period as particularly bad for American civil liberties and free speech. There was a great anti-German hysteria with German churches and publications closing or changing to English. Some suspected German insiders were lynched. 

America entered the war in 1917, and German success in the East precipitated the Russian revolution (the socialists wanted OUT of the war). The war was ruinous for Russians, and socialism was diverse and changing: not all socialists wanted to overthrow democracy by force and instead act through parliament. Russia didn't have democracy. Lenin and Trotsky were exiled after the failed revolution of 1905. The Bolsheviks enjoyed popularity offering to withdraw at all costs and they passed a (pretty bad) treaty with Germany; but the czarists and democracy supporters (white Russians) were in opposition. Britain and the U.S. sent troops to aid the white Russians, which helped established a bitter dislike of the Americans and British. 

After a slow start for the US, soon Americans (laying mines and using the convoy system to avoid subs built up numbers) and British pushed the Germans back and forced the signing of the peace treaty. 

Wilson travelled to Europe for peace talks and Herbert Hoover organized famine relief. Wilson presented the 14 points for lasting peace including the surrender of all conquered lands, opened treaties (no secret diplomacy), democratic self-determination for minority groups, an international League of Nations to settle disputers. The Kaiser had abdicated and the German republic supported the 14 points as their best hope. 

Wilson was disillusioned by vengeful, blameful punitive reparations towards Germany (John Maynard Keynes saw the treaty as foreseeing another war and resigned from British govt.). Germans were dismayed by Wilson's reluctant acceptance of the British and French treaty, and were in such a weak state (undergoing revolution and domestic unrest), that they had no choice but to accept (they had to give away land, break up the Austro-Hungarian empire). 

Wilson was dismayed (and kind of failed), but hoped to use the League of Nations to aid in this new world order (and address his perceived faults in the treaty), but the Senate thwarted US membership: they saw small nations as overrepresented and Wilson had failed to consider congress in negotiations even though his party had lost in the midterms and didn't take any Republicans to Versailles. Wilson suffered a stroke, but was never able to finish negotiations and the League was compromised from the start. An epidemic (the Spanish Flu) had swept through the country

The Russian revolution intensified anti-radical fears and prompted the (first) red scare. Many Americans were horrified by the possibility of a revolution. Anarchists hadn't helped, sending out bombs in the mail and the AG (Palmer raids) violated due process and deported radicals without it (he actually later appointed J. Edgar Hoover). Socialism had already been weakened by jailing of its leaders and the splitting of the party. The "peaceful transition" socialists were discredited when socialists across the world did fight in the war for patriotism, and the third international dedicated socialists to the overthrow of capitalism by force. The Russian socialists claimed the authority on socialists policy and direction and this split American socialists (follow Russian lead as communists vs not supporting Russian violence policy).
