
## Carter

An active evangelical, born-again Christian, he was the Governor of GA able to present himself as uncontaminated by Washington. He was controversially honest and frank, particularly in one interview with Playboy. 

He defeated Ford in 1976 and made human rights central to foreign policy; he withdrew American support for the Sha of Iran and the Nicaraguan dictator over human rights violations. He proposed peace in the middle east an successfully arranged the Camp David Accords between Israel and Egypt (totally unprecedented) to agree on a basic treaty (Egyptian Sadat was assassinated for it). Carter oversaw the relinquishment of the Panama Canal which barely passed congress. 

Carter's economic policies were hampered by stagflation due to oil politics. Oil prices were rising due to the OPEC cartel restricting supply, and it percolated every facet of the economy. This led to imports of foreign cars and Chrysler went bankrupt.

Carter faced a primary in 1980, but was mostly ended by the Iranian Revolution. Students seized the embassy in Iran and held captives; this was done in retaliation for letting the exiled Sha receive medical treatment in the US for cancer. Ayatollah Khomeini had returned in triumph as the new dictator (had obviously authorized the seizure), and crowds chanted death to America and burning effigies of carter. Carter authorized a mission to capture the hostages but it failed miserably and was called off, underlying the feeling of American impotence (particularly following Vietnam). 

The USSR had their own Vietnam in Afghanistan, poisoning detente. The pro-soviet regime needed more and more support over time and escalated. Carter cut off trade with the USSR, angering American farm grain exporters, and prohibited athletes from participating in that years Olympics in Moscow. The Senate rejected the second strategic arms limitation talks. Instead of peace, tensions were going up. 

Iran waited until Reagan's inauguration to release the hostages as an insult to Carter and Reagan appointed Carter as his envoy to meet the hostages. 

## Reagan

His victory in 1980 indicated a shift in the Republican party. The religious right supported Reagan over Carter who didn't try hard enough to roll back moral changes: pro-family legislation, prohibit abortion, work against ERA and Reagan would in their minds. Ever since the 1920s Scopes Monkey Trial, the evangelicals hadn't had a major role in politics. Conservative republicans had been crushed in 1964 but had been working to organize since then and were happy with Reagan. 

Reagan was famous from radio, movies and television and Hollywood. He was relaxed and charismatic and not high strung like many fundamentalists. Where Carter was unlucky, Reagan was lucky. He immediately survived an assassination attempt basically making him an immediate God. Republican idealogues were occasionally dismissed during scandals but Reagan was never really affected. The environmental deregulation scandal in his EPA. 

In 1984, Mondale was lackluster and Jesse Jackson (running on civil rights) wanted to build a rainbow coalition to move national policy leftwards. The democrats were crushed. 

Reagan abandoned bipartisan consensus, espousing the supply-side revolution: by cutting taxes, you gamble that more money in the economy accelerates growth and actually generates more tax revenue; this was advanced by Milton Freedman. Republicans had an awkward coalition between pro-family types who wanted federal intervention in family policy but libertarians want minimal government, who gained more under Reagan.

Reagan found it very difficult to diminish the scale of government. Meanwhile he wanted to address the cold war, and increased the military budget substantially (increasing deficits). He raised the Missouri, campaigned against leftists in Granada, described the USSR as the "evil empire". His militant posture against the USSR was compounded by aggressive policy in Latin America, supporting anti-communist guerillas (contras) in Nicaragua and favored the El Salvador regime fighting off left guerillas. Carter failures had been that when you abandon a dictator, their successor might be worse, so Reagan administration distinguished between authoritarian and totalitarian regimes. 

The Star Wars program planned to militarize satellites in space to shoot down Soviet missiles which was expensive and provocative. Reagan's Teflon image enabled him to endure the Iran-Contra scandal. Congress had prohibited American aid to the Nicaraguan Contras (Bollander Amendment), but Reagan sold weapons to Iran (an enemy) and used the money to support the Contras. Nicaraguan government shot down a CIA plane carrying weapons to the Contras and a survivor confessed what was happening leading to a great scandal. Reagan was clearly involved but was mostly unaffected and congress did not want to impeach him. The press always treated him much better.

## The New World Order

The fall of the USSR was a surprise to the US: everyone thought they were perfectly capable of weathering hard times. Non-Russian nationalities and Eastern Europeans had grown weary of the USSR following their Afghanistan debacle. American relations improved towards the end with a new weapons treaty and when the soviets withdrew from Afghanistan, but by then it was too late for them. Gorbachev had begun Perestroika and Glasnost: to open the USSR to access information and the second to create economic incentives instead of the command economy. With new IT, controlling information had become too difficult. The Polish had become particularly resistant and finally the Iron Curtain collapsed in '89 and Gorbachev announced that Eastern European nations were allowed to determine their own government. The Berlin Wall was brought down by students. Bush was careful not to gloat and instead to shore up democracy. The same year, the Chinese cracked down on their own demonstrations and the US was reluctant to respond to keep trade good. 

The cold war had finally ended. The US was left as the sole superpower without a nuclear war. The first test was in 1990, when Saddam Hussein (Iraq) invaded Kuwait to the south. Until then, it had seemed beneficial to supply the Iraqis with weapons to fight the Iranians. Kuwait was a major oil producer however, and now that the Cold War had ended, Bush took the view that medium sized nations can't bully smaller ones and wanted to set a precedent. First they imposed sanctions, then UN forces began air raids, then there was a ground invasion as last resort and the war was a decisive victory for the US. There was a period where Iraw had been firing missiles at Israel and there was a fear in the administration that Israel would intervene against Iraq and antagonize US Arab allies in the region. The media restricted TV access in response to Vietnam. Bush decided not to overthrow Saddam; later some people regretted this when Saddam used chemical weapons against the Kurds. Also the military had returned to volunteers and morale was better. 

The next issue was in the Balkans. Communism had temporarily repressed ethnic and religious antagonisms under Tito and once it ended the wars continued with horrible ethnic cleansings. No one wanted to enter in NATO as another contender, there was no clear target or objective for armies to intervene and no one wanted to be imperialist. The Rwanda Genocide faced similar issues and little intervention. 

International anti-American terrorism created new anxieties. Groups started hijacking aircraft for publicity and leverage. Usually it was benign and hostages were released. In 1993 the World Trade Center was bombed by Islamic fundamentalists who showed little remorse. A guy who hated the government bombed a building in Oklahoma City. Terrorism was hugely costly in time, security, and money. Security businesses did well. If the terrorists were willing to lay down their own lives for their cause it would be difficult to stop and that's what happened on 9/11. The US was far safer after the Cold War since nuclear war was no longer a threat, but it didn't feel safer. The USSR had kept the lid on in many places and now war was popping up all over and enemies were harder to identify- they didn't correlate with nation-states and sometimes were even US citizens. 

Regardless, the US was left the only superpower and what they did mattered; the cultural radiance around the world was enormous and even people who couldn't afford to immigrate loved the culture. 

## Clinton's America and Beyond

Clinton was very skilled but had great weaknesses. He was a gifted campaigner, elected to Arkansas governor at 32. He was a chameleon and a great empathizer. But he had dodged the draft and was a rumored philanderer. He also worked against much of LBJ's great society. 

In 1992, everyone thought Bush was headed for re-election, having just won a war; but there was a recession that year and he had broken a promise not to raise taxes. James Carville had a sign in the campaign "it's the economy, stupid". The field was pretty clear since major democrats thought Bush was so strong. Ross Perot took advantage of voter indignation and ran as third party. It's not obvious, but he probably took more votes from Bush. 

Clinton pushed controversial policies: get rid of "don't ask don't tell", and a national healthcare system which he assigned to Hillary and she failed miserably at. The US finished the century without a right to healthcare: "America has the best healthcare in the world for those who can afford it"; and this means medical research is geared towards resolving the sufferings of rich people. Healthcare attached to jobs tended to freeze people in place. 

Dislike of Clinton led to great Republican victories in 1994 and for the first time in 50 years controlled both houses of congress. Newt Gingrich promised the "contract with America" to balance the federal budget, introduce term limits for congressman, and improved ethics in Washington. He was a poor negotiator and failed to shrink the government; the government shutdown (lmao people blamed Republicans). 

By 1996 the economy was doing very well and Clinton was reelected easily. His second term was hampered by the Monica Lewinsky affair. He and Hillary had been under investigation for investment fraud (the whitewater affair), but there was no smoking gun. Sexual harassment claims were made against Clinton and then he had the affair. He lied about the affair on TV and in Washington. He was impeached for perjury and obstruction of justice. He did admit it and say it was wrong eventually. For the first time, the media covered and described the sexual scandal. The good economy and poor perception of the case led to victory in the midterms and he was not convicted. 

Bush Jr. won in 2000 after the narrow recount in Florida where the ballot was confusing. The wealth gap continued to grow from the 90s. A tech bubble grew and made people fabulously wealthy; but technology did get better. 

The 90s failed to succeed in the war on poverty, and reforms were passed in 1996. Bastard children were much more common amongst the poor since the 1960s, intensifying the problem of the feminization of poverty. A belief grew that welfare actually hampered the poor. Charles Murray's *Losing Ground* perpetuated this, and *The Bell Curve* which claimed that genetic differences in intelligence caused this and that African Americans were generally less intelligent. Some people thought there were Gay genes or Alcoholic genes. Eugenics style arguments started to come back. 

Affirmative action debates continued, and SCOTUS interpreted the law more conservatively over time, rolling back the programs. Blacks were split between a thriving middle class and a big, demoralized underclass in inner cities. There was large scale rioting following the Rodney King murder. In the 90s, crime and prison rates were much higher than in the rest of the world. The death penalty was highly debated as well. 

The 90s was an era of culture wars. It claimed a crisis in the defense of Western Society, horrified by the "assault" on "dead white males". 9/11 emphasized continuing instability and a wealth gap in between countries. The Bush administration decided to take a proactive foreign policy and attacked Afghanistan to oust the Taliban, the regime which defended Al Qaeda. The victory was nebulous, the terror networks having not been destroyed; and Americans couldn't replace the regime with a stable government. In 2003 the US attacked Iraq claiming WMDs and that it was wise to strike first (Saddam and Iraqi government behavior didn't help). Most of America's allies (apart from Great Britain) didn't want to enter a political quagmire and arise more anti-American sentiment, also being unable to replace Saddam's despotism. The US almost immediately crushed the Iraq government but again the ambiguous aftermath played out with no obvious replacement. Despite American military power, it could not preside of a harmonious world with domestic consensus about its place in the world.
