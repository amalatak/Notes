## The Roaring 20s

The first world war had made clear the U.S. was a major world power, but yet it didn't engage much on the world stage. Instead it had restricted immigration, it passed prohibition leading to organized crime and souring opinions on the law, intolerant protestants had revived the KKK to target Blacks, Catholics, and Jews. There was high employment, improving cities, rising wages, and the spread of technology to ordinary families. Art had also prospered. 

Following the immigration surge of the 19th and early 20th century, Americans feared displacement and generally changed to be less favorable towards immigrants. They passed laws restricting immigration from southern and easter Europe and banned it from Asia (of course allowing Anglo-Saxons), a mark of racialism of the time. 

Prohibition, at the time had its reasons. Alcohol contributed to domestic abuse, marital rape, public intoxication, accidents at work; and temperance involved women in mass for the first time. The war helped, with grain being seen as more necessary for the war and many brewers were German. It ended up poorly enforced by the treasury and it removed quality control and taxes (eventually these were used to end prohibition), but puritan ire still disagreed with permitting an immoral act; soon it gave way to organized crime, bootleggers, and apathy at prohibition laws. The bible belt saw some success. 

The KKK went through a revival advocating for white supremacy, specifically WASP. They persuaded Oregon to pass a law mandating public schools, eliminating Catholic schools. Society of sisters protested, and SCOTUS found in favor of them establishing that states can mandate education but not a monopoly on it. They didn't like a "wild and dangerous youth culture". Fundamentalists banned teaching Darwinism (Scopes Monkey Trial) in Tennessee; they won the case but lost great PR. 

New technologies came out routinely, liberating women with labor-saving machines in house work. Per-capita income was very high, but consumerism was criticized by some (Babbitt, Great Gatsby). Hollywood movies became big. 

There was a new youth culture: women cut their hair short (bob) and wore shorter dresses (sometimes smoking and drinking in public), shocking old conventions. Freud claimed repressing sexuality contributed to dissatisfaction, and puritanism was scorned. Jazz music became popular and spread to middle class. 

Following the war, women and black men (great migration away from the south) began working in industrial cities; women were rewarded with the vote. This had no affect on election outcomes, horrifying older suffrage leaders (in tandem with new liberation movements). Blacks migrated Northward away from Southern sharecropping, leading to White Flight in many cities from neighborhoods that blacks moved into, leading to black ghettos such as in Harlem (which also lead to the Harlem Renaissance with first black identities). After the war, demobilized white veterans saw them as threats to their jobs and there were a series of race riots through black neighborhoods. Marcus Garvey lead a "back to Africa" campaign, rejected by some other black leaders (W. E. B. DuBois).  

Exceptionally, the size of the government was shrinking at a time of totalitarianism in Italy, Germany, Russia, and other European countries. 

## The Collapse

Minimal stock regulation and unsound investment practices created unrealistic expectations and speculation. Share prices collapsed in 1929 and ruined many. Its causes were maldistribution of income and over the next four years a downward spiral of economic shrinkage, factory closings, bankruptcies, and unemployment. The dust bowl didn't help, and Hoover became a scapegoat. 

The boom conditions of the 20s lead to misleading ideas and speculative bubbles. In 1926, Florida land values plummeted overnight as a premonition after growing rapidly for vacation and retirement. Mosquito suppression made development more possible regarding malaria (this also allowed for the Panama canal). Speculators could buy worthless land and still sell it at a profit. In 1926 two hurricanes swept through and crushed the speculation, collapsing values. 

Investment trusts allowed investors to buy on margin very easily. This amplified gains but also losses. Dividends were low, discouraging long term holding. The Fed didn't do anything to restrain this either. People thought that the Federal Reserve was supposed to protect them from economic swings. The actual share of people invested was very small but their disastrous losses rippled throughout the entire economy. 

There's not actually strong evidence as to what caused the depression; it's thought that overproduction and wealth inequality contributed. There were also high tariffs at the time, and the world economy was suffering from Germany's economic stagnation in the wake of Versailles. 

Hoovervilles" and hobo camps began popping up. Layoffs and wage cuts reduced aggregate demand and worsened the problem. A drought in the west returned leading to the dust bowl and bankrupting farms. 

Hoover took the brunt of the blame for the depression. His actions were too little too late and his tactless governing did not comfort the American people (see the veterans bonus protest). He did engage in public works, but rejected the idea of direct involvement by the federal government.

## The New Deal

FDR swept in with bold political experimentation. He attempted to reduce competition, and created a variety of federal agencies definitively putting more power in the federal government instead of the states. SCOTUS nullified a bunch of them, but the nation loved it. Father Charles Cofflin used radio to criticize; and Huey Long wanted to oust him. Roosevelt attempted to pack SCOTUS in 1936, probably overstepping. 

Roosevelt was a blue blood, very privileged. He was paralyzed by Polio after being the Secretary of the Navy but recovered (the press tended to hide his disability). It's thought that this made him more patient, shrewd, and determined. He was much better able to unite the Solid (white) South and the ethnic working class groups of the North. 

Roosevelt was a pragmatist willing to try just about anything. He didn't give out direct payments, but, with congress, he created a ton of agencies: alphabet soup e.g. National Recovery Act (NRA) to reduce competition by price fixing (it also recognized unions collective bargaining), Public Works Administration built a bunch of infrastructure, Civilian Conservation Core (CCC) hired 3m young men to build trails and parks and do agricultural work, agricultural administration bought farm excess.

The Emergency Farm Mortgage Act forestalled bankruptcies on farms and the Home Owners Loan Act did the same for regular homeowners. Banks were regularly failing and there were bank runs everywhere: the Glass-Stiegl Banking Act created the FDIC to guarantee ordinary citizens' banking deposits. The Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) directly built damns to generate hydro-electric power (contrasted with Hoover who didn't want to do this). FDR pushed to suspend prohibition and it was repealed soon after. 

He used the radio (fireside chats) to sell his policies and comfort Americans. The Social Security Act (1935) transformed old age which had been a period of chronic anxiety; now the government provided a pension and security to the elderly. It also included unemployment insurance. The National Labor Relations Act strengthened unions and boomed its participation. Unions also did new things: they started sit-downs; sitting in the factory itself (where replacements would normally go) instead of outside where they could be removed easily and their spouses provided support from outside. FDR intervened on behalf of the unions. 

By 1935 SCOTUS had rejected the NRA and the AAA; in 1936 FDR faced opposition especially that Father who favored Catholic social justice (believing in a Jewish banker conspiracy); Huey Long was a populist governor of LA and then a congressman who wanted a universal basic income (assassinated). Francis Townsend wanted everyone over 60 to retire and in return they would get payments they had to spend. Membership in the communist party peaked during this time (still small though).

FDR crushed the election. Wealth Republicans hated him (they passed a soak-the-rich tax). He tried to reshape SCOTUS (packing it with favorable appointees and retiring old ones) but that was a third-rail. Saved by the bell, one of the justices began voting in favor anyway (a switch in time saves 9) and one more retired freeing up an appointment. 

The New Deal instilled confidence in the nation but didn't actually solve the depression. Rearmament for WW2 ended up doing that. 

## World War II

### Background 

Hitler's rise to power caused growing alarm in the US, and his attacks on neighboring countries and Jews drove the US closer to GB and desiring to intervene. Roosevelt gave covert aide to the British until the attack on Pearl harbor. The US began to rapidly mobilize but the prospect of victory seemed remote. Anti-Japanese panic lead to internment camps. 

### Europe

Germans regarded the Versailles treaty as unjust and it compromised the legitimacy of the Weimar Republic. Hitler took power and abolished the constitution. An intense cult of personality developed around him. He persecuted communists and Jews, with the final solution to eliminate Jews taking priority even to supplying troops at times. 

The League of Nations, lacking American presence was powerless against aggressors of Japan in Manchuria, Italy in Ethiopia, and the Spanish civil war. Hitler signed an alliance with Stalin in 1939 shocking the world since they were typically opposed. Hitler usurped territories all the while reassuring Western leaders of peaceful intent, while they were desperately eager to believe him (period of appeasement; Churchill vs Chamberlain). They thought WW1 was caused by too much eagerness for war. 

Hitler's Blitzkrieg overpowered Poland, then Norway and Denmark, Belgium and France. Britain declared war after Poland and built up armaments in France while Germany invaded Poland but didn't invade. Following that period of nothing (the phony war), Hitler toppled France and Flanders in weeks. The British fled continental Europe and the Battle of Britain began (aerial bombings of England). 

Steadfast British resistance lead by Churchill swung American public opinion in favor of Britain. Churchill did everything in his power to get the US involved but FDR was very cautious. The US did send goods and some naval destroyers (something about lend lease) in exchange for use of some bases in the Caribbean; the US coast guard escorted export supply ships with food and other goods to prevent submarine attacks in the North Atlantic. But the American public was cautious of the violence from WW1; many Americans were isolationist or were skeptical of German atrocities (as they had rushed to assume the worst in WW1).

In the summer of 1941 Hitler shocked the world again by invading the USSR (eventually the downfall for Germany- their supply lines were too long and the winters were too harsh).

Roosevelt and Churchill agreed to go after Germany first while Stalin pleaded with them to invade to launch an early second front. Churchill was quite content to see them go at each other (they had been allies after all so he didn't trust Stalin, and Hitler was struck by the Allied unity). Germany was well defended and they wanted to build up supplies for an invasion first; also bombing was thought to weaken Germany and demoralize the people. Many planes were shot down. It had the opposite affect, and rallied German people. Further, they moved industry to the countryside, and bombs were inaccurate. Many German cities were destroyed and some German refugee cities were controversially bombed (Dresden).  

So the soviets bore the brunt of the German assault until D-day. Americans did ship supplies through the North Sea to Russia, over which the battle of the North Sea was fought to keep open. Allied code breakers had broken Enigma fairly quickly and soon after the triton code which raised ethical concerns over when to intervene. 

The Italians tried to conquer North Africa where they faced British opposition. Rommel lead the forces but was defeated at El Ale main. American forces pushed East from West Africa and eventually they united to push up from Italy where the mountains made it difficult to take. Meanwhile American and Royal bombers targeted German industry. Trans-Atlantic convoys braved submarine attacks and concentrated supplied in England for D-day (second front). 

The major invasion was launched in Normandy as a surprise and it worked. The night before, paratroopers blew up crucial bridges to the beach and the navy bombarded the coast just before the invasion. Despite losing the war, the Germans fought ferociously. Germans disguised as Americans sewed confusion and caused mistrust (Americans used media to quiz people).

The big three leaders met to discuss what would be done with Germany after the war. Stalin wanted to keep Eastern Europe to prevent a third German invasion.
### Pacific

Pearl Harbor changed everything. Japan had been growing in influence: it had been the first Asian nation to industrialize, had won victories against the Russians in 1905, and was expanding in China. They realized the US was a Pacific rival (given growing influence after the Spanish-American war). They believe democracy had eroded "martial virtues" and gone soft. They thought they could knock out the US with a sufficient, decisive blow, knowing the US was a much bigger economy.

It back fired and immediately galvanizing support in favor of war to the death with Japan (isolationists were silenced). They had sunk a bunch of warships, but missed the submarines and aircraft carriers which were not in port at the time (those proved deadly in that theater). Further it was shallow waters and many were raised. Within two days, Germany declared war on the US. Roosevelt saw Germany as the bigger threat (more industrial). 

There were invasion scares in California and allegations were made that Japanese-Americans were spies (even ones that had been there for generations). In late 1941 and 1942, the government rounded them up to internment camps (in the mountains or deserts) without any evidence of sedition. They were forced to sell their property at a steep loss. The American media reported on it spinning it both ways. Conditions were not good, more like a prison. 

In 1941/42 the US suffered great defeats. In Clark Field, Japanese destroyed many aircraft and the US surrendered and abandoned the Philippines after a Japanese invasion (no backup was available after Pearl Harbor). The prisoners were forced to march to POW camps at Baton (Baton Death March), galvanizing the public. Japan regarded surrender as dishonorable and weren't signatories to the Geneva convention (which specified how to treat POWs), leading to bitter national and racial resentments. McArthur promised "I shall return". 

The do-little raids were implemented in response from carriers to let Japan know that the mainland was potentially vulnerable. It wasn't destructive but it horrified their high-command. 

Aircraft carriers became critical, such as the Battle of Midway (fought without fleets even seeing each other) and Coral Sea (may have forestalled an Australian invasion) was fought. Code breakers had figured out that Midway was planned to conduct a second Pearl Harbor (Americans used Navajo). Americans were able to repair and manufacture ships extremely quickly. 

Progress over islands was slow but tenacious. The strategy was to take islands and establish air bases to attack subsequent islands, shipping lanes, and the mainland. Nimitz favored jumping directly towards Japan, McArthur favored a southerly hopping land route. The U.S. quickly established naval and air superiority so Japan struggled to resupply its islands and it was too spread out; some particularly tricky islands were just bypassed and "left to wither on the vine". When islands were invaded, they desperately fought to the last man; and when defeat was imminent they bayonet charged into fire and occasionally citizens committed suicide. 

After Saipan was taken, the mainland was in range (Tojo resigned after it was taken). American casualties were smaller due to technical superiority. Prior to invasion, battleships and planes would bombard an island. The battle of Lady Gulf was fought.

As the war approached the mainland, resistance stiffened. Iwo Jima (famous flag raising photo) and then Okinawa (Kamikaze) featured heavy casualties. Japan prepared a civil defense force of all ages on the mainland (some literally had sticks). From 1944 onwards, the US constantly bombed the mainland (Tokyo fire bombings). 

Once Europe was done, it wasn't clear the Pacific was nearing an end. Estimates had huge casualties before the Japanese surrendered. Physicists (Fermi) had been studying controlled nuclear reactions with potential military application. The president was notified when there was a success ("the Italian navigator has landed in the new world"). The Manhattan project was created without congressional knowledge of any details (also ironically developed by many Germans/Jews). US/British scientists thought that the enemy could develop one first and German scientists had split an atom in 1939. The first test succeeded in NM in 1945. 

Newly inaugurated Truman had to make the decision. They knew an invasion would be very costly, but some advisors wanted to make a demonstration of the bomb before dropping it. Truman doubted that could happen and there were only two left. He concluded to use it as is. Hiroshima and Nagasaki were bombed. Japan surrendered unconditionally except that the emperor would be retained (who announced the end of the war) on the Missouri. McArthur became the commander inside Japan to rebuild and create a democracy to replace the military autocracy.  
### Homefront

The draft and rearmament ended the depression. Many many men were rejected on health reasons; huge numbers of Americans simply never went to the doctor. 

The economy boomed during the war and women moved into factories. It was seen as temporary at first since there was poor daycare and many women had children. Marriages also exploded prior to deployments, which often ended in divorce or bereavement. Kaiser was a famous ship builder. Wealth inequality decreased, wages increased, and healthcare wildly improved. Healthcare insurance schemes began. 

Anti-Japanese propaganda was common, "Japs" were shown as vicious and racially comical. Because of Pearl Harbor and the Death March and mistreatment of war prisoners, Japanese were more hated than the Germans. 

Roosevelt won a fourth term in 1944 with a new VP. Roosevelt died soon after and Truman had little knowledge of the Manhattan Project. When Truman was inaugurated it was one of the first things he was told about (and that it was almost done).

The US emerged from the war an economic powerhouse and undamaged at home.
