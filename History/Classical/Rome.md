## Origins

The origins of Rome are unknown. The city was sacked in 386 BC, removing any founding documents. When Troy is sacked, Anneas escapes to modern Rome location (some thing about sewing the seeds of the punic wars). Anneas got in some battles, won, and set the Tiber river between Latins and Etruscans (origins unknown, maybe came from Anatolia; they controlled much of the Italian peninsula at this time). 

The Etruscans had a city and state-system, while the Latins were tribal herdsman until the founding of Rome. Etruscans had trading routes to Greece straight through Rome (probably helped its founding). Latin culture was Greek and Etruscan, but only excelled in Engineering and Warfare. Latins were bound by their language.

## Legend

Anneas' son became king, who founded a new city that became powerful. His great grandson Numitor was King and his brother coveted the throne and chased him out of the city. His daughter was raped, thrown in prison, and birthed two sons who were to be drowned in the Tiber river, but they were left on the banks. They were nursed by a she-wolf and picked up by a herdsman who raised Romulus and Remus. 

R&R raided brigand camps, and the robbers set a trap and captured Remus, who was presented to the landlord (the exiled Numitor) who recognized them. The herdsman told Romulus who hatched a plan with Numitor to take back the city. They each led an army and succeeded. 

The brothers left to found a new city on the spot they were abandoned, but had a falling out over who would lead the new city. They waited on different hills and then fought. Remus was killed. There's also a wall story. In any case, there was Rome. 

Eventually Romulus opened the city to the dregs of society, which ended up being mostly men. They couldn't coax women to marry, so they stole them particularly the Sabine women. They fought the Sabines over this and eventually reached a peace. 

Romulus eventually disappeared in a thunderclap leaving a power void, wherein the first interlude of a gap between rulers was observed with Senators ruling for a day. 
## The Seven Kings

Rome was then ruled by seven kings alternating between religious and warlike. The warlike king is supposed to have destroyed a city after the three-brother thing to decide battle. Apparently the losers betrayed the Romans and let the Etruscans in, but Rome defeated them anyway. They pushed populations into Rome and then pushed Romans to settle the outer cities, growing until they got a port. 

At this point, Rome was a warrior society tampered by Religion.
