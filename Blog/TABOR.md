The Taxpayers Bill of Rights restricts government spending in Colorado since the 1990s. Some are calling to kill it while many defend it. I understand each argument for this but ultimately come down on the side of killing TABOR for several reasons.

First and foremost, it simply doesn't work as intended in practice. TABOR might work better in one of those economic models that assume every voter is a rational actor, not unlike physics equations that assume spherical objects in a vacuum; but obviously, people are not rational actors. Since its implementation, voters have never once voted to raise taxes, save for sin taxes. I find it, solely based on outcome given the pandemic, three financial crises, and booming population very hard to believe rational actors would not have increased taxes during a single one of those phases to address, at a minimum, temporary measures such as unemployment increases, housing increases, road funding, etc. As a result, we have a failing education system, a failing infrastructure system, a failing healthcare system, the list goes on. 

This will not be a discussion on the effectiveness of government and wasteful spending (which may come later), but for now we will assume the "you get what you pay for" model.

Another issue is that this is double-hitting government's core function of taxing and spending.
